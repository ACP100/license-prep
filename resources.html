Tips collected from these sources

  1. Cover all 10 chapters. Each is worth 10 marks, so skipping one costs a lot. Chapter 10 (drawing, economics, CPM/PERT, professional practice, NEC Act) is mostly memorisation and an easy place to pick up marks.
  2. Practise the numericals: cache address fields and effective access time, Booth's multiplication, IEEE 754, K-maps, number conversions, 8085 addressing modes and interrupt vectors, page replacement, CPU scheduling, tree traversals and quicksort partitions, 2D/3D transformation matrices, NPV/IRR and CPM float.
  3. Chapters people flag as weak: Computer Organization & Embedded Systems, DSA, and Software Engineering.
  4. Learn the laws Chapter 10 asks about: NEC Act 2055 (amended 2079), NEC Regulations 2057, Public Procurement Act 2063, Electronic Transactions Act 2063.
  5. Do the last two or three exams' questions to learn the style: your project's questions-from-memory docs first, then the GitBook and necmcq sets.
  6. Take timed 100-question mocks on a computer. The exam is computer-based, and pacing matters more than people expect.
  7. Never leave a blank. There's no negative marking.
  8. Check answers in community banks. They contain mistakes: NECPrep's own validator reports defects, some GitBook links are broken, and the BCT past-questions PDF has a wrong effective-access-time solution.
